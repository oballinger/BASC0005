Explore it: clustering without an answer key

Six months of radar detections with no AIS match, off the Belgian and Dutch coast: the input to the DBSCAN step in the lecture. DBSCAN returns clusters at any setting you give it. Change the radius ε and the minimum number of points and watch the "structures" it finds appear, split, merge and vanish. Nothing in the unlabelled data says which answer is right. Then reveal the answer key (Global Fishing Watch's published structure locations) to score your choice: the check that supervised learning has and clustering usually doesn't.

Every radius gives an answer

Data. Structure locations are real: Global Fishing Watch offshore infrastructure (public table v20231106, CC BY-NC 4.0), wind turbines present in the first half of 2021. The detections are simulated around them for the Week 10 workshop (about 15 per structure, a few tens of metres off, plus randomly scattered unmatched vessels and loose pile-ups from anchored ships). Distances use a flat local projection. A cluster counts as correct if its centre is within 150 m of a structure, each structure matched once; the patch is clustered with a 1 km buffer so edges behave. Tap or hover a detection to see why it got its label.

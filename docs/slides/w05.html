Explore it: the Central Limit Theorem

Take many random samples from a population and work out the mean of each. Those sample means pile up into a bell curve centred on the true mean, whatever shape the population has, and the bell gets narrower as the sample gets bigger. Pick a variable from the lecture, set the sample size n, and draw samples to see it happen.

Population: the 2013 respondents in the Current Population Survey extract from the Week 5 workshop, treated as the whole population as in the workshop (union: the 8,830 people asked the union question, 1 = member; income: wage earners under $200k, rounded to the nearest $1k). Samples are drawn at random, with replacement, in your browser. SE = standard error = σ/√n, the spread the Central Limit Theorem predicts for the sample means. Source: U.S. Census Bureau, CPS.
